AidChain | Bringing Light to Humanitarian Aid

## ✨ What is this?
Hey there! 👋

We built AidChain because we got tired of hearing stories about aid money getting lost in bureaucracy.

This is our hackathon attempt to fix that.

It's a transparent donation platform where every dollar is tracked, every project has real photos (pulled live from Pexels), and donors can actually SEE where their money went.

Check out the live demo here

   ## 🤔 The Problem 
Working on this project made us realize three things:

Donors feel disconnected - You send money and... nothing. No feedback, no photos, no "thank you" from the people you helped.

Paperwork eats funding - Administrative costs shouldn't swallow 50% of every donation.

Trust is broken - Too many scandals, too little transparency.

We wanted to build something that feels different. Something that proves aid can be honest.

## 💡 What We Built
Here's what you can actually DO on this site:

🏠 Impact Terminal (The Donation Hub)
Fill out a quick form - name, country, what sector you care about (education, medical, water, etc.), and how much. Hit the button. Watch your donation appear in the live tracker.

We added an anonymous option too. Sometimes you just want to help without the spotlight.

🎯 Active Deployments (Real Projects, Real Photos)
We have used with Pexels API to pull REAL photography of:

Solar water wells in Somali regions

Vaccine delivery to mountain villages

Rural tech hubs with actual laptops

Mobile maternal clinics

Each project has a description, sector tag, and clickable images. You can cycle through 8+ photos per project WITHOUT reloading the page (we're pretty proud of that feature).

📊 The Ledger (Who Gave What)
Every single donation shows up here - name, amount, country, timestamp. No hiding. No "disappearing funds."

We kept anonymous donors hidden by choice. Privacy matters.

🌙 Dark Mode? Of course.
We're night coders. Dark mode was non-negotiable.

🛠️ How We Built This (The Tech Stack)
We kept it simple because hackathons are supposed to be about solving problems, not fighting frameworks:

Plain HTML/CSS/JavaScript - No React, no Vue, just vanilla stuff that works

Pexels API - For real humanitarian photography (with photographer credits - respect the artists!)

LocalStorage - So your dark mode preference doesn't disappear on refresh

CSS Grid + Flexbox - Because responsive design shouldn't be painful

The whole thing is static - no backend, no database, no payment processors. This is a prototype to prove the CONCEPT, not a production app (yet).

# 🎨 Design Choices (Our Thought Process)
Why gold?
Gold represents value, trust, and something worth protecting. Felt right for humanitarian aid.

Why parchment/blue-black themes?
Light mode feels warm and approachable (like paper documents). Dark mode feels trustworthy and secure (like a bank vault). Both have their place.

Why card-based layout?
Each project deserves its own spotlight. Cards let people browse naturally.

Why Pexels instead of stock photos?
Stock photos feel fake. Pexels has real photographers capturing real moments. That authenticity matters when you're asking people to trust you with their money.
